# Dungeon Progression, Difficulty, and Economy

This is the canonical authority for dungeon pressure, unsecured rewards,
extraction and death outcomes, hub economy, preparation costs, and expedition
energy economy. The roster split, expedition assignments, categories, and
reunion loop are defined in
[Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).
Individual level and stat advancement are defined in
[Character Growth and Stat Scaling](character-growth-and-stat-scaling.md).

## Core Principle

Dungeon Drifters should always allow the player to enter and play dungeons.

Dungeon runs do not consume energy.

Expeditions consume a regenerating energy resource.

As the player advances, dungeons become increasingly punishing. Continued progress requires stronger characters, improved equipment, better party composition, more resources, and deeper knowledge of dungeon mechanics.

The player can overcome this pressure through:

* active dungeon grinding
* expeditions
* strategic party rotation
* equipment upgrades
* crafting
* improved character builds
* better dungeon preparation
* optional paid acceleration

The game does not stop the player from playing.

It pressures the player to decide how they want to progress.

---

# 1. The Two Progression Paths

The player always has two primary ways to improve the roster.

## Active Dungeon Grinding

The player directly enters dungeons and earns progress through gameplay.

Dungeon grinding provides:

* full experience for the active pair
* common materials
* currency
* equipment drops
* consumables
* combat practice
* dungeon knowledge
* relationship development
* occasional rare rewards

Dungeon grinding costs time and attention but does not consume energy.

A player who does not want to spend money can continue progressing by playing more dungeon runs.

## Expeditions

Expeditions are the supporting progression path alongside directly played
dungeon runs. Their assignment, categories, reward resolution, and character
experience distribution are defined in
[Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).

Expeditions consume energy. The player may wait for it to regenerate, spend
earned resources to restore some energy, or purchase optional acceleration.

---

# 2. Expedition Energy

Expedition energy is a launch resource tied only to the expedition system. Once
an assignment launches, it advances through gameplay events rather than a
real-world countdown; see the
[party and expedition contract](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).

It does not prevent the player from:

* entering dungeons
* fighting enemies
* advancing character relationships
* testing party combinations
* collecting normal dungeon rewards
* grinding experience manually
* attempting difficult content

When expedition energy is empty, the player can continue playing Dungeon Drifters.

The limitation is that the inactive pair cannot begin another full expedition until energy is available.

This creates pressure without creating a complete play barrier.

---

# 3. Why Expeditions Matter

Expeditions are not optional background flavor.

They become increasingly important as the player moves into harder regions.

Early dungeons can be cleared with basic leveling and ordinary equipment.

Later dungeons require more specialized preparation.

The player may need:

* resistance equipment
* upgraded weapons
* rare crafting materials
* stronger consumables
* character-specific gear
* knowledge of enemy weaknesses
* hidden routes
* boss preparation items
* improved expedition rewards
* a more balanced roster

Expeditions provide faster or more targeted access to these resources than ordinary dungeon grinding.

The player can still grind manually, but expeditions reduce the amount of repeated dungeon play required.

---

# 4. Progressive Dungeon Pressure

Each new region should increase difficulty through more than higher enemy statistics.

## Increased Enemy Strength

Enemies gain:

* more health
* stronger attacks
* improved defenses
* more complex abilities
* better coordination
* resistance to simple strategies

## Environmental Punishment

Later dungeons introduce conditions such as:

* poison
* extreme heat or cold
* flooding
* darkness
* unstable terrain
* magical corruption
* limited healing
* mana disruption
* equipment degradation
* persistent injuries

## Resource Attrition

The player must manage:

* health
* mana
* consumables
* durability
* status effects
* limited rest opportunities
* retreat decisions

## Character Matchups

Certain enemies and environments heavily favor particular Drifters or party combinations.

Using the wrong pair remains possible, but requires:

* better equipment
* higher levels
* stronger consumables
* greater player skill
* additional grinding

## Roster Requirements

Story dungeons may require specific characters.

Bosses may require the whole roster to remain viable. The active/expedition
split and full-party convergence are defined in
[Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).

---

# 5. Soft Progression Walls

Dungeon Drifters should primarily use soft walls rather than hard power gates.

A hard wall prevents entry.

Example:

> Party level too low. Entry denied.

A soft wall allows entry but makes the consequences clear.

Example:

> The enemies resist your primary damage types, the environment drains mana, and your equipment lacks corruption resistance.

The player may still attempt the dungeon.

A skilled, prepared, or stubborn player might succeed early.

Most players will respond by:

* grinding levels
* changing party members
* upgrading equipment
* crafting resistance items
* completing expeditions
* gathering information
* changing tactics

Soft walls preserve player agency while still supporting progression pressure.

---

# 6. Difficulty Curve

## Early Game

The early game teaches the full system.

Characteristics:

* most pairings are viable
* enemies have simple weaknesses
* expeditions are inexpensive
* energy feels generous
* gear upgrades are straightforward
* level differences remain small
* mistakes are recoverable
* dungeon grinding produces meaningful progress quickly

The player learns:

* how each Drifter works
* how active and expedition parties interact
* how equipment affects combat
* how dungeon traits influence party choice
* how expeditions support progression

## Early Midgame

The game begins asking the player to make deliberate choices.

Characteristics:

* dungeon weaknesses matter more
* some characters begin falling behind
* basic equipment becomes insufficient
* expeditions provide useful targeted materials
* party synergy becomes important
* careless resource use causes failed runs

The player begins rotating characters and planning deployments rather than simply selecting favorites.

## Late Midgame

The progression economy becomes central.

Characteristics:

* dungeons require specialized gear
* rare materials become necessary
* enemy mechanics punish poor matchups
* expedition selection affects future dungeon success
* underdeveloped Drifters create problems in required story content
* boss preparation may require several dungeon and expedition cycles

The player can still grind normally, but targeted expeditions save substantial time.

## Late Game

Late-game progression demands full system mastery.

Characteristics:

* strong party specialization
* dangerous environmental effects
* layered enemy mechanics
* demanding equipment requirements
* limited room for poorly developed characters
* full-party bosses that test the complete roster
* high-value expeditions
* rare crafting resources
* difficult optional dungeons

The player must understand both combat and roster management.

## Endgame

Endgame content supports long-term play.

Possible systems include:

* rotating challenge dungeons
* elite expeditions
* rare gear sets
* advanced crafting
* high-difficulty bosses
* seasonal modifiers
* prestige progression
* score-based dungeon runs
* restricted party challenges
* endless dungeon modes
* account-wide cosmetic rewards

---

# 7. The Progression Decision

When the player reaches a difficult dungeon, they have several options.

## Push Forward

Attempt the dungeon immediately.

Advantages:

* possible early victory
* no delay
* greater challenge
* potential access to higher-tier rewards

Risks:

* failed run
* wasted consumables
* injuries
* lost resources
* slower overall progression

## Grind Dungeons

Repeat available content manually.

Advantages:

* unlimited access
* full active-party experience
* direct control over progression
* no expedition energy required
* additional practice

Costs:

* greater time investment
* repeated content
* slower access to targeted rare materials

## Run Expeditions

Send the inactive pair after specific resources.

Advantages:

* targeted rewards
* passive roster experience
* gear and material acquisition
* scouting advantages
* reduced manual grinding

Costs:

* expedition energy
* delayed resolution
* lower experience than active dungeon play

## Change Party Composition

Bring characters better suited to the dungeon.

Advantages:

* reduced need for raw statistical power
* access to better counters
* new pairing synergies
* more efficient progression

Costs:

* weaker or less familiar characters may require training
* equipment must be redistributed
* the preferred pair may be temporarily separated

## Improve Preparation

Craft items, change equipment, study enemy information, or unlock new abilities.

Advantages:

* rewards planning
* reduces reliance on raw level grinding
* supports different playstyles

Costs:

* consumes resources
* may require previous expeditions or dungeon runs

---

# 8. Expedition Energy Economy

The energy economy should be generous enough that expeditions feel like part of normal play.

It should not feel as though the game immediately demands payment.

## Energy Regeneration

Energy regenerates over time.

Possible sources of additional energy include:

* daily login rewards
* story progression
* dungeon milestones
* boss victories
* achievements
* level rewards
* rare dungeon drops
* limited free refills
* optional purchases

## Expedition Costs

Expedition costs may vary based on:

* expedition length
* danger level
* reward quality
* destination
* selected characters
* special modifiers
* guaranteed rewards

A common material expedition may cost little energy.

A rare artifact expedition may consume a significant portion of the player’s energy capacity.

## Energy Capacity

Energy capacity may increase through:

* account progression
* hub upgrades
* story milestones
* achievements
* optional permanent purchases

Capacity increases should provide convenience without making the base system unusable.

# 10. Monetization Loop

The monetization loop is built around expedition acceleration, not access to dungeon gameplay.

## Free Player Loop

A free player can:

* play unlimited dungeons
* earn full experience manually
* collect normal materials
* wait for expedition energy
* use earned energy refills
* rotate characters
* grind difficult content
* complete the story
* defeat bosses
* develop the entire roster

Progress may be slower, but it remains fully possible.

## Paying Player Loop

A paying player may choose to:

* refill expedition energy
* increase energy capacity
* complete more expeditions
* gain targeted materials faster
* reduce required grinding
* accelerate roster development
* purchase cosmetic content
* purchase optional convenience features

Payment improves efficiency and reduces repetition.

It does not replace playing the game.

---

# 11. Monetization Boundaries

The system should avoid making payment feel mandatory for basic play.

The player should not be required to pay to:

* enter normal dungeons
* continue the main story
* use a specific Drifter
* participate in boss battles
* unlock essential mechanics
* recover from ordinary failure
* obtain basic progression materials
* keep the game playable

The strongest monetization targets are:

* expedition energy refills
* increased energy capacity
* additional expedition options
* expedition reward bonuses
* cosmetics
* camp decorations
* character skins
* visual effects
* optional passes
* convenience bundles

Direct combat power should be handled carefully.

A paid advantage that completely bypasses dungeon preparation would undermine the central gameplay loop.

---

# 12. Failure and Recovery

Failure should encourage adaptation rather than abandonment, but dungeon death
has a specific extraction consequence: all unsecured rewards from that run are
lost. See **Secured and Unsecured Progress** below for the complete rule.

Other unsuccessful outcomes may involve spent consumables, temporary injuries,
forced retreat, expedition opportunity cost, or delayed boss progress. Do not
interpret those possibilities as partial retention after death; escape methods
have their own outcomes under **Extraction Methods**.

After returning to the hub, the player may grind, run an expedition, change
characters, craft equipment, gather information, improve abilities, or try
another dungeon.

---

# 13. Example Progression Wall

The player reaches the Ashen Vault.

Known conditions:

* enemies resist physical damage
* heat causes persistent health loss
* healing items are less effective
* the dungeon contains magical armor

The player’s preferred team is Branoc and Zhaivra.

They attempt the dungeon and fail.

The player now has several options.

## Option One: Grind

Run previous dungeons with Branoc and Zhaivra to gain levels and collect more consumables.

## Option Two: Rotate

Bring Azhvielle for stronger magical damage and pair her with Branoc for protection.

## Option Three: Expeditions

Send Zhaivra and Joruun on a material expedition for heat-resistant crafting components.

## Option Four: Scout

Complete a scouting expedition to reveal a hidden route that avoids the worst environmental damage.

## Option Five: Upgrade

Use existing materials to craft heat-resistant armor and magical damage consumables.

A free player can solve the wall through additional play.

A paying player may accelerate the expedition portion.

The dungeon remains playable in either case.

---

# 14. Boss Preparation Loop

Bosses should represent the highest point of progression pressure within a region.

Before a boss, the player may need to:

* train all four Drifters
* complete character-specific dungeons
* gather boss weakness information
* craft resistance equipment
* recover consumables
* upgrade abilities
* complete special expeditions
* locate the boss arena
* weaken environmental hazards
* distribute equipment across the full team

The whole roster may be required for the boss. Its party format and convergence
are defined in [Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).

---

# 15. Complete Mobile Progression Loop

The party selection, expedition assignment, reward split, and reunion contract
are defined in [Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).
From the economy perspective, the complete loop becomes:

```text
Prepare at the hub
-> choose a dungeon and review its strengths, weaknesses, and restrictions
-> enter and play the dungeon
-> accumulate unsecured experience, gold, and loot
-> decide whether to continue or extract
-> return successfully and secure eligible run rewards, or die and lose them
-> resolve the other pair's expedition
-> level characters, craft and improve equipment, and redistribute resources
-> grind, rotate, prepare, or run more expeditions as difficulty rises
-> prepare the full roster for the region boss
-> clear the boss and unlock the next region
-> repeat at a higher level of pressure
```

---

# 16. Core Economic Identity

Dungeon Drifters does not monetize the player’s ability to play.

It monetizes acceleration within the roster-management economy.

The player can always enter dungeons.

The player can always grind.

The player can always improve through active play.

Expeditions provide a faster, targeted, and more convenient progression route.

As dungeons become increasingly punishing, expeditions become increasingly valuable.

The central economic tension is:

> Do I spend more time grinding the resources I need, or do I use expedition energy to acquire them more efficiently?

That decision supports both the gameplay loop and the mobile economy.

---

# 17. Secured and Unsecured Progress

The game distinguishes between progress already secured at the hub and rewards
currently carried inside the dungeon.

## Secured Progress

Secured progress includes things already returned to the hub or permanently
applied:

* previously secured character levels
* previously banked experience, depending on the final leveling model
* signature-weapon refinement
* installed weapon components
* equipped accessories
* stored items
* banked gold
* completed story progress
* permanent unlocks

Secured progress is not lost when the party dies during a later dungeon run.

## Unsecured Progress

Unsecured progress includes rewards earned during the current dungeon run:

* experience
* gold
* weapon components
* refinement materials
* alchemical ingredients
* consumables found inside
* rings and pendants
* rare loot
* other dungeon rewards

Unsecured rewards become secured only after a successful extraction, except
where a specific extraction method defines a different outcome below.

## Death Rule

If the active party dies inside the dungeon:

* all unsecured experience is lost
* all unsecured gold is lost
* all unsecured loot is lost
* all unsecured components and materials are lost
* the party returns to the hub
* previously secured progression remains intact

The player loses the current run, not the entire save file. The player does not
lose previously secured levels, the Drifters, signature weapons, installed
weapon components, equipped accessories, banked gold, stored items, or completed
story progress. No corpse-retrieval system is currently intended.

The important risk decision occurs before death:

> Continue deeper for greater rewards, or leave while the current run can still
> be secured?

This exact death outcome takes precedence over general statements that failure
should encourage adaptation or that a failed attempt might retain rewards.

---

# 18. Extraction Methods

## Normal Extraction

The party reaches an authorized exit, dungeon completion point, or other valid
extraction location.

Result:

* keep unsecured experience
* keep unsecured gold
* keep unsecured loot
* convert the run's rewards into secured progress
* no special item cost

This is the ideal outcome.

## Smoke Bomb

A smoke bomb provides a guaranteed escape from the current combat encounter.

Intended result:

* immediately end or escape the active encounter
* preserve the active dungeon run
* remain inside the dungeon
* consume the smoke bomb
* require the player to still reach an extraction point or use another escape
  method

A smoke bomb is not a full dungeon extraction item. It prevents one dangerous
battle from automatically destroying the run.

## Return Talisman

A return talisman immediately removes the party from the dungeon.

Intended result:

* preserve unsecured experience
* abandon unsecured gold
* abandon unsecured loot
* abandon unsecured materials and components
* consume the talisman
* return directly to the hub

This creates an emergency decision: preserve the experience earned during the
run, or risk everything while trying to leave normally? The talisman should not
function as a free perfect extraction. Its exact name remains unresolved.

# 19. Hub Trader and Supplies

The trader is the central preparation and recovery service at the hub. The
trader should provide reliable access to useful, story-appropriate supplies
without selling the strongest rewards in the game.

## Regular Stock

Possible regular stock includes:

* healing potions
* smoke bombs
* return talismans
* common alchemical ingredients
* common weapon-refinement materials
* basic weapon components
* simple resistance items
* ordinary utility consumables
* preparation supplies

The trader should ensure that core character mechanics remain usable without
depending entirely on random drops.

## Story and Dungeon Progression

Trader inventory should expand according to:

* story progression
* dungeon depth
* defeated bosses
* discovered regions
* unlocked trade routes
* rescued or encountered NPCs
* recovered crafting knowledge

The trader may sell equipment appropriate to the current stage of the game. The
trader should provide reliable adequacy; the dungeon should provide exceptional
power.

## Alchemical Supplies

Zhaivra's core compound mechanics should not depend completely on random
dungeon drops. The trader should sell common ingredients required for basic
preparations, such as common fire and poison compounds, smoke-producing
ingredients, basic reaction agents, and reservoir-filling materials.

Rare, transformative, or unusually powerful reagents should still come from
dungeon exploration, elite enemies, bosses, rare gathering points, dismantled
equipment, hidden rooms, or story rewards.

```text
Trader -> dependable access to the basic kit
Dungeon -> rare ingredients and build-defining discoveries
```

Exact compound-consumption rules remain unresolved. Alchemy remains separate
from weapon component progression.

## Healing Potions

Healing potions should be purchasable from the trader, discoverable inside the
dungeon, obtainable through rewards, and subject to a carrying limit. A limit
keeps excessive stockpiling from becoming the dominant solution to dungeon
difficulty.

Preparation should require meaningful choices between more healing, an
emergency smoke bomb, a return talisman, alchemical supplies, weapon-refinement
materials, accessories, and saving gold for later progression.

---

# 20. Gold

Gold becomes the primary hub-economy currency. Intended gold sinks include:

* healing potions, smoke bombs, and return talismans
* alchemical ingredients
* common refinement materials and weapon components
* rings and pendants
* component-installation and component-removal services
* other future crafting or preparation costs

Gold found during a dungeon run remains unsecured until successful extraction.
Banked hub gold is secured while gold found during the active run is at risk;
the exact rules for gold carried into a dungeon remain unresolved.

---

# 21. Accessories

Each Drifter should have a small accessory system separate from their permanent
signature weapon.

Proposed slots:

* one ring
* one pendant

Two slots provide meaningful build customization without turning the characters
into generic armor mannequins or undermining their signature equipment.

## Trader Accessories

The trader may sell useful accessories appropriate to the current story and
dungeon tier. Possible effects include increased maximum HP or mana, improved
potion effectiveness, elemental or poison resistance, improved accuracy or
dodge, modest critical bonuses or stat bonuses, improved searching, faster
super-meter generation, or reduced consumable costs.

Trader accessories should be useful and dependable, but generally not the
strongest available items.

## Dungeon Accessories

The strongest, strangest, and most build-defining rings and pendants should
come from exploration, bosses, elite enemies, hidden rooms, rare events, story
choices, or major discoveries. The player may buy a competent accessory; the
player should usually discover a legendary one.

Previously equipped rings and pendants are secured equipment and are not lost
when the party dies during a later dungeon run. An accessory found during the
current run remains unsecured until extraction. Rules for equipping newly found
accessories inside the dungeon remain unresolved.

---

# 22. Risk and Preparation

The system should create a recurring choice between spending secured resources
now and saving them for later.

Before entering the dungeon, the player may decide to buy additional healing,
buy an escape tool, purchase basic alchemical supplies, buy common weapon
materials, purchase a story-appropriate accessory, save gold for future
refinement, or enter underprepared to preserve resources.

Inside the dungeon, the player repeatedly decides whether to continue deeper,
search another room, fight an optional encounter, retreat toward an exit, use a
smoke bomb, sacrifice loot with a return talisman, or risk losing the entire
unsecured run.

---

# 23. Relationship to Expeditions

The active dungeon run and the expedition system remain distinct.

Dungeon runs are directly played, create extraction risk, produce unsecured
experience and loot, and may be entered whenever story rules permit.

Expeditions are assignments using expedition energy; they gather supporting
resources and help underused Drifters remain viable. They may provide common
supplies, ingredients, or materials. Their assignment, event-based completion,
categories, and rewards are defined in
[Party and Expedition Core Loop](party-and-expedition-core-loop.md).

Exact overlap between expedition rewards and trader stock remains unresolved.

---

# 24. Design Principle

The trader does not replace dungeon exploration. The trader provides
preparation, reliability, recovery, basic supplies, common progression
materials, and level-appropriate equipment.

The dungeon provides danger, experience, rare components, powerful accessories,
exceptional reagents, major weapon development, and the highest-value rewards.

The death system gives those rewards meaning by placing the current run at risk.

---

# 25. Explicitly Unresolved

Do not freeze these yet:

* trader identity and lore
* trader inventory interface
* exact prices and gold drop quantities
* exact healing values, potion prices, carrying capacity, and potion tiers
* inventory limits and storage rules
* whether banked experience exists before leveling and exact leveling location
* exact return-talisman name and whether talismans work during combat
* whether smoke bombs work against bosses or can be countered by certain enemies
* smoke-bomb carrying limit, turn cost, and whether use is automatic or selected
  before defeat
* exact accessory effects, rarity system, and whether accessories can be upgraded
* reward scaling and balancing formulas
* whether some loot is automatically secured
* whether story items can ever be lost or quest items are exempt from death loss
* exact expedition and trader reward overlap
* exact rules for equipping newly found accessories inside the dungeon
* exact compound-consumption rules

Story-critical items and permanent unlocks should not be lost through ordinary
dungeon death unless explicitly designed otherwise.
